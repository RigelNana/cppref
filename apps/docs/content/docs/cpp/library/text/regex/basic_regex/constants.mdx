{/* source:cpp-regex-basic-regex-constants:0001 */}
[`std::basic_regex`](/docs/cpp/library/text/regex/basic_regex) defines several constants that govern general regex matching syntax.

{/* source:cpp-regex-basic-regex-constants:0002 */}
These constants are duplicated from `std::regex_constants`:

{/* source:cpp-regex-basic-regex-constants:0003 */}
<DescriptionList>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`ECMAScript`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Use the [Modified ECMAScript regular expression grammar](/docs/cpp/library/text/regex/ecmascript).</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`basic`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Use the basic POSIX regular expression grammar ([grammar documentation](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799/basedefs/V1_chap09.html#tag_09_03)).</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`extended`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Use the extended POSIX regular expression grammar ([grammar documentation](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799/basedefs/V1_chap09.html#tag_09_04)).</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`awk`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Use the regular expression grammar used by the awk utility in POSIX ([grammar documentation](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799/utilities/awk.html#tag_20_06_13_04)).</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`grep`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Use the regular expression grammar used by the grep utility in POSIX. This is effectively the same as the `basic` option with the addition of newline `'\n'` as an alternation separator.</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`egrep`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Use the regular expression grammar used by the grep utility, with the -E option, in POSIX. This is effectively the same as the `extended` option with the addition of newline `'\n'` as an alternation separator in addition to '|'.</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
</DescriptionList>

<DescriptionList>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`icase`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Character matching should be performed without regard to case.</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`nosubs`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>When performing matches, all marked sub-expressions (`expr`) are treated as non-marking sub-expressions (?:`expr`). No matches are stored in the supplied [`std::regex_match`](/docs/cpp/library/text/regex/regex_match) structure and mark_count() is zero.</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`optimize`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Instructs the regular expression engine to make matching faster, with the potential cost of making construction slower. For example, this might mean converting a non-deterministic FSA to a deterministic FSA.</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`collate`</DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Character ranges of the form "[a-b]" will be locale sensitive.</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
  <DescriptionItem>
    <DescriptionTerm>`multiline` <InlineRevision since="C++17" /></DescriptionTerm>
    <DescriptionBody>Specifies that ^ shall match the beginning of a line and $ shall match the end of a line, if the ECMAScript engine is selected.</DescriptionBody>
  </DescriptionItem>
</DescriptionList>

{/* source:cpp-regex-basic-regex-constants:0004 */}
At most one grammar option can be chosen out of `ECMAScript`, `basic`, `extended`, `awk`, `grep`, `egrep`. If no grammar is chosen, `ECMAScript` is assumed to be selected. The other options serve as variations, such that `std::regex("meow", std::regex::icase)` is equivalent to `std::regex("meow", std::regex::ECMAScript|std::regex::icase)`.
